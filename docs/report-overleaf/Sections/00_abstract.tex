\begin{abstract}
Day-ahead electricity price forecasts can have large errors under conditions
that aggregate accuracy measures obscure. We study which conditions are
associated with large errors in western Denmark (DK1).
Using price and weather data from 2019--2024, the monthly Ridge benchmark
produces forecasts for 17,544 delivery hours in 2023--2024, refitting each
month on an expanding window containing only earlier days.
K-means defines daily market regimes, and Apriori selects associations
on 2023 errors for evaluation in 2024. Ridge mean absolute error was
18.78 and 18.34 EUR/MWh in the two years. In 2024, the autumn-evening
rule had a lift of 1.88, while the selected cluster-afternoon rule had
a lift of 1.02. Within afternoon hours, cluster 0 had a large-error rate
of 9.16\% versus 11.28\% for other clusters. The difference is uncertain
and applies to the selected rule and period. The autumn-evening rule
also remained enriched for gradient boosting in 2024, with a lift of
2.19, whereas the cluster-afternoon rule had a lift of 0.91.
These results provide partial evidence of cross-model persistence
for the calendar association, while the selected cluster condition
did not consistently identify higher error rates.
\end{abstract}
