\section{Discussion}
The autumn-evening association persisted across linear, tree-based,
and neural forecasts. Lasso and Ridge have closely correlated errors
(0.98 by hour), so transfer to the other model families broadens the
evidence. These associations support monitoring autumn evenings.
Causal effects of season, time, and market regime remain open.

Annual accuracy measures capture different aspects of forecast error.
Ridge's MAE fell from 18.78 to 18.34 between years, while its RMSE
rose from 26.65 to 29.52 and its eligible hit rate fell. Likewise,
boosting's lower annual MAE in 2024 came with somewhat higher MAE
on negative prices than Ridge. An error-monitoring rule can therefore
add information beyond the annual model ranking.

The selected cluster label added little enrichment within afternoons.
The Davies--Bouldin index measures separation of inputs, and sparse
high-price regimes make errors difficult to characterize. The rare
regime also falls below the 5\% Apriori support threshold. Its high
forest and boosting error rates warrant cautious interpretation.
Comparing regimes within the afternoon band holds time of day fixed.
Seasonal composition can still differ, so the comparison cannot
isolate the contribution of the cluster label alone.

Both years informed earlier work. The 2024 results therefore give a
chronological development evaluation. Intervals condition on the
selected rules, and uncertain source release times limit claims
about the operational 10:00 cutoff.
The five forecasts share input data and evaluation hours. Transfer
across model families gives evidence against a pattern tied to one
model's specification, while a prospective evaluation is needed to
assess whether it persists under later market conditions.
